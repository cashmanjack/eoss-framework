## A. L2 write against read cost (plain CUDA, plain events plus ncu)
Kernel: every thread issues a fixed count of independent 4 byte or 32 byte accesses with L1 bypass (ld.global.cg / st.global.cg), warp uniform choice of read or write by a fixed pattern, addresses pseudo random but warp aligned inside a footprint, no data dependence (reads summed into a register stored once). Patterns: coal (a warp touches 128 contiguous bytes, 4 sectors, 4 B per lane), lane4 (each lane its own 32 B sector, 4 B access: partial sector writes, like the heap with 128 B per query strided across lanes), lane32 (each lane its own sector, 32 B access as two 16 B stores: full sector writes). Write share of accesses 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1. Footprints 8, 32 and 56 MB (all inside the 72 MB L2; 56 MB tests the edge region). 3 repetitions, 25 blocks of launches discarded as warm up, medians. Metric: time per L2 sector (sectors requested / duration), from events, and ncu lts__t_sectors_op_read and _write, lts__t_sector_hit_rate, lts__throughput pct for one run per config.
Predictions:
1. L2 hit rate at least 98 percent in every config at 8 and 32 MB: 85 percent.
2. Read only L2 sector rate (lane32 at 32 MB) between 3 and 6 TB/s: 55 percent; coal read only between 3 and 6 TB/s: 55 percent.
3. Write cost ratio c_w / c_r (time per sector, write only over read only), lane4 at 32 MB: between 2 and 8: 55 percent; above 6.8 (the post hoc F419 figure): 20 percent; below 1.5: 15 percent.
4. Partial sector writes (lane4) cost more per sector than full sector writes (lane32): 60 percent; coal writes (4 sectors per request, full sectors) cost about the same per sector as lane32 (within 20 percent): 55 percent.
5. Mixes are linear: the 50 percent write mix time per sector is within 15 percent of the mean of the two ends (same pattern, 32 MB): 55 percent.
6. Footprint does not matter inside the L2 (8 against 56 MB, time per sector within 15 percent, every pattern and mix): 60 percent.
7. Rule for use in C: c_r and c_w are taken from the pattern closest to the kernel's heap traffic: lane4 for the original's stores and loads (a 128 B heap row per query, 4 B element updates), coal for the register heap's traffic; if lane4 and coal differ by more than 30 percent per sector in c_r, I report both predictions in C and say which pattern fit the byte mix better, without choosing after seeing the speedup (the pattern is chosen now: lane4 for both builds as the primary prediction, coal for both as the secondary).

## C. Prediction check: register heap speedup from the byte mix and measured write and read costs, per chip
Method (written now, applied to the 4090 today, to the 5090 when its numbers exist): per round and in total, T(build) = R x c_r + W x c_w, with R and W the L2 sectors read and written by the build (ncu lts__t_sectors_op_read, _write; the 4090's from the second ncu run of session 6, F419) and c_r, c_w the time per sector from A (pattern lane4, 32 MB, primary; coal secondary). Predicted speedup = T(original) / T(register heap). No fit to the kNN data. Compared with the measured kernel speedups (3.9 to 5.5 per round, 4.2 to 4.5 total) and with the pure byte ratio (2.8 to 3.65) and the post hoc F419 fit (not used).
For the 5090 the byte mix (R, W) was not measured there; the scorer uses the 4090's write share per build per round with the 5090's own byte totals (F417: 576 and 199 MB in round 1, ... 9,557 and 3,527 MB in round 4), says so, and uses the 5090's c_r and c_w from its microbenchmark. That is a prediction made without the 5090's write share, so a miss may come from the mix, not the costs; both are stated then.
Predictions:
1. 4090 total (rounds 1 to 4) predicted speedup within 15 percent of the measured kernel speedup (4.2 to 4.5): 50 percent; within 30 percent: 80 percent. Per round within 25 percent in at least 3 of 4 rounds: 60 percent. Closer to the measured value than the pure byte ratio in every round: 75 percent.
2. Rounds 1 and 2 are overpredicted (the register heap does not saturate the L2 there: 73 and 77 percent of peak): 65 percent.
3. 5090: if the 5090's write over read cost ratio (lane4) is below the 4090's by at least 30 percent, the predicted 5090 total speedup is below the 4090's and within 25 percent of the measured 3.17: 55 percent. If the ratios are equal within 30 percent the model predicts about the 4090's speedup and misses the 5090: then the cause is not the write cost, and F419's reading is wrong for Blackwell (stated as such).
