## Answers
1. Does the kernel speedup match the L2 byte ratio on the 4090? NO. The byte ratio is the same as on the 5090 (2.8 to 3.65 against 2.7 to 3.6) but the kernel speedup is 1.40 to 1.52 times the byte ratio in every round (3.9 to 5.5), against 1.03 to 1.10 on the 5090.
2. Why Ada 4.0 to 4.5 against 3.08 on Blackwell: the byte ratio and the instruction counts are chip independent (2.7 to 3.6, 0.9 to 1.05); what differs is how fast each build can pull bytes through the L2. On the 4090 the original is at 91 to 95 percent of the L2 peak with only 1.4 to 1.6 TB/s (its traffic is 68 to 75 percent writes) and the register heap reaches 2.1 to 2.2 TB/s (27 to 39 percent writes), so the speedup is byte ratio x 1.5. On the 5090 the two rates differ by only 1.10 (3.07 and 3.38 TB/s, both under 80 percent of peak). So the Ada speedup is the byte ratio times a write cost factor that is about 1.5 on Ada and 1.1 on Blackwell. Why Blackwell's write cost factor is smaller is NOT tested: no 5090 access here, and its write share was not measured (suggested: run the metric list of ttu/bench/Phase10f/ncu_4090_batch6b.sh on pc14 with its sm_120 paths; prediction: write share of the original about 70 percent as here, byte ratio as before, and a smaller per sector cost gap).
Caveats: the explanation rests on 2 repetitions of 4 rounds, an 8 point 2 parameter post hoc fit, and one input (uniform 200,000 points, radius 0.004); DRAM is not involved (at most 15 percent of peak, L2 hit 94 to 100 percent), so the original is limited by the L2 write path, not by memory.
