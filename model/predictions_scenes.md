Observations about the predictions themselves (before measuring): for camera rays the model predicts almost no gain from any sorted order (the pixel order is already coherent; the replay's hit tables for oracle depths are identical on Conference and Sponza) and a small slowdown for shuffling (0.95 to 0.99); the only set with a predicted gain from sorting is Rungholt bounce (oracle full 1.10, Morton 0.97).
Written expectations: (1) camera rays: the F123 form UNDER predicts the cost of shuffling a coherent order (measured shuffled slowdown larger than predicted, at least 10 percent below 1.0 on two of three scenes; the Memory_4 form's 0.67 to 0.75 is nearer); probability 65 percent. (2) Sorted orders of camera rays measure 1.00 to 1.04 (the pixel order is already near the best order). (3) Bounce rays: Sponza and Conference within 0.12 of the measurement; Rungholt bounce UNDER predicted for the deep oracle orders as for random rays in F205 (measured oracle full above 1.2 against the predicted 1.10), error 0.10 to 0.25. (4) Against the baseline of 1.0: the model beats it on pooled error over the 42 points only because of the shuffled and Rungholt bounce points; on the oracle orders of camera rays both are nearly the same; I expect pooled model 0.08 to 0.15 against baseline 0.10 to 0.20 (low confidence).
Reading rules (written now): error per set = rms of log(predicted / measured) over the 7 orders; spread (= the baseline's error) = rms of the log measured speedups; pass for a set at most 0.12 (as F205); the model is "better than no change" on a set if its error is below the spread. Pooled errors over 42 points for the model, the Memory_4 form and the baseline. No refit and no retuning after the measurement; a refit would be a separate labeled step. Camera rays: Morton uses direction (different from the origin code of the bounce rays), stated. Measure step: scene_check.py measure, 10 launches per order, median, every result compared with the natural order result field by field ('wrong' must be 0), raw files timestamped.
