Verdict against the rule set before the run (model with L_unl within a log error of 0.22 in frames 0 to 4 and 9): FAILED, log error -0.97 and -1.19. Even with the loaded latency (L_ld, which contains the queueing) the model is 0.047 to 0.082 ms against 0.100 to 0.246 measured (log error -0.75 at frame 0 and -1.1 at frame 9). The callback term does NOT close the gap.

What was ruled out
- Callbacks: they are 7 to 23 percent of the summed warp time (11 percent at frame 0, 23 percent at frame 5, 7 percent at frame 9); 73 to 92 percent of the warp time is the traversal window (trace window minus callbacks), 0 to 7 percent other. The callbacks are light (loaded 1,100 to 5,000 cycles per warp call, unloaded 700 to 780; the loaded to unloaded ratio falls from 6.4 at frame 0 to 1.6 at frame 9).
- The hand off term: 0.002 to 0.014 ms.
- Probe overhead: spans of the probed run equal run B (probes off) within 0.006 ms in every frame (0.1004 against 0.1055 at frame 0, 0.2458 against 0.2386 at frame 9): the probes are free here.
- Counts: the probe warp calls and the replay's warp calls agree within 0.5 percent at frames 0 to 4; the probe counts are 37 percent above the replay's from frame 5 on (a different quantity: the replay counts the maximum over the lanes of a warp, the probe counts leader lane entries of the active mask, and lanes that call at different times are counted separately); F166's per query counts are right.
- The clock: 3.1 GHz instead of 2.5 would lower the model's cycles to ms conversion by 20 percent, not by the factor 2 to 3 needed.

What the probes show instead: the time is in the traversal window, a node fetch chain plus queueing, not in the program. (1) One warp per SM (4,544 queries): the trace window is 10,136 cycles at frame 0 (8.6 nodes per warp maximum) and 121,644 at frame 9 (267.8); fit over the 142 warps of a frame: 380 to 430 cycles per node of the warp's maximum node count plus 12,000 to 20,000 cycles fixed (frames 5, 7, 9; correlation 0.33, 0.53, 0.77; at frame 0 the maximum varies too little to fit), the F67 cost per node (343) and a fixed part larger than the 740 to 1,640 of one query: this is a latency chain, not throughput. (2) Loaded: raygen cycles per warp are 7.0 times the one warp value at frame 0 (78,339 against 11,205) and 3.6 times at frame 9 (441,834 against 122,691); implied warps in flight 6.9 at frame 0 (of 22 warps per SM) rising to 15.8 at frame 9. (3) Node fetches per SM per launch (replay nodes times 704 queries per SM) over the span in cycles: 57 cycles per node at frame 0 (4,400 nodes in 251,000 cycles), 8.2, 4.5, 3.5, 3.4 at frames 6 to 9 (35,000 to 179,000 nodes), so it is neither a constant node throughput nor, at frame 0, near the 0.153 rays per cycle per TPC of F105.

Not explained: the 14 times between the unit capacity bound and the measured span at frame 0 (a launch of 100,000 queries with 6 nodes and one candidate each takes 100 microseconds, 355 cycles per query per SM; the F127 constant c0 = 110 was fitted on launches with 10 to 228 candidates and F127 already says c0 fails at the smallest radius); whether the time is first touch and launch ramp (3,125 warps over 142 SMs: implied warps in flight 6.9 of 22 resident, so most warps start late or wait), queueing in the traversal unit, or the memory system; a model for it would need a launch ramp term and a chain term (about 400 cycles per node of the warp maximum) that no one has tested on a loaded launch. Stopped here as instructed.
