# Rungholt residual and DRAM traffic: test plan and expectations (2026-11-17, tethys; CPU only, written BEFORE any DRAM feature was compared with the F205 residuals)

Honest status: the F205 measurements and the replay histograms (notes/hs/reuse_rungholt_*.txt, predict_rungholt.json feature D) already exist, and I have seen the F205 residuals (F123 form under predicts all five oracle orders, -0.119 to -0.168, growing with depth; Morton -0.030). I have NOT computed any DRAM quantity against them. No GPU run, no refit of any F123 parameter. This is a test of an already fitted model against a candidate cause, labeled as such.

Candidate cause (F205): the F123 form has no DRAM term (its fitted weight was zero), so it under counts the cost of the natural order on a 416 MiB BVH (5.8 times the L2), which would make the model under predict how much an order that cuts DRAM traffic gains.
Feature: D per order = 128 B x (cold + overflow node fetches of one SM stream) per ray x max(0, 1 - L2/BVH), the existing predict step feature (node lines only, no triangle lines, L2 hit share is the crude 1 - L2/BVH). x_i = log(D_natural / D_i), the DRAM traffic reduction of order i.

Tests (all on the 5 oracle orders, Morton as a 6th held out point):
- T1, no parameters: DRAM bytes per launch = D x 8,388,608 rays; bandwidth floor time = bytes / 1008 GB/s (4090 spec); compare with the measured ms of each order. If the floor is below 30 percent of the measured time for every order, DRAM bandwidth cannot be what the residual is made of (latency of the misses is a different thing, tested by T2 only as a correlation).
- T2: residual r_i = log(measured speedup / F123 predicted speedup). Spearman rank correlation of r_i with x_i; one slope b through the origin (r = b x), in sample rms of r - b x and leave one out rms; the Morton residual predicted by b x_Morton.
- T3, the null: the same T2 with the order index (depth 1 to 5) and with the L1 miss reduction log(F_nat/F_i) in place of x_i. D explains something only if it beats both.
Pass for "DRAM traffic explains the residual" (all three needed): leave one out rms of r - b x at most 0.06 (half of the 0.124 mean signed residual, about the F123 noise), Spearman at least 0.8, AND better leave one out rms than both T3 regressors, AND the Morton residual predicted within 0.04.
Expectations (written now): bandwidth floor is small (I expect 10 to 40 percent of measured, so T1 says bandwidth alone is not it); with 5 points all three monotone regressors will correlate with the residual (the depth order and the traffic reduction both grow with depth), so I expect D NOT to beat the order index clearly and the overall verdict to be "not shown, 5 points cannot separate the candidate from depth"; probability that all pass 25 percent. If the verdict is "not shown" it is reported as that, not as a failure of the DRAM idea.
