## What the trace shows
- Every session's answer: 434 candidate answers (tag 0x50, hit flag 1) and 26 empty answers (tag 0x7f); the words R2, R30, R31, R62, R63, R68, R69 are zero in all 460 answers. So an answer carries no distance, no second candidate and no extra state outside the pointer, the instance word echo and the two pending entry pairs.
- (a) tmax: REFUTED as the cause. The kernel never changes tmax (R17, R31 are only reloaded from the record) and no answer word holds a t.
- (b) stop rule ends a lane while state remains: 20 of 24 bad lanes end on an empty answer after a candidate whose pending entries were fed back (the unit says it is done); 4 bad lanes end on a candidate answer with no pending entry (the stop rule ended them). Not separable from the trace alone which of those 4 lost candidates at the end rather than earlier. Open, but the 20 lanes that ended on an empty answer did not lose their tail to the stop rule.
- (c) inactive lanes lose resume state: NOT supported. All 32 lanes are active in sessions 1 to 6 and 31 or more to session 9, yet the first OptiX neighbour absent from our list is at position 0 to 4 of OptiX's order in all 24 bad lanes (position 0 in lanes 3, 6, 18, 19): the loss starts early with a full warp. Lane 15 (the longest, 23 sessions, alone for the last sessions) is exact; the other exact lanes have 6 to 23 sessions.
- Where the divergence first happens, per lane: not identifiable from the trace alone. The trace has the record index of each session but not the primitive, and the OptiX harness logged only neighbours in call order, not every candidate; mapping records to primitives needs the GAS record table (not saved). What the data give: our neighbour list equals OptiX's with entries missing in between (lane 3: OptiX 2884, 2241, 3259, 1178, 431, 3688, 2121; ours 2241, 1178, 431, 2121), never a truncated tail; the deficit of sessions against OptiX candidates is 1 to 8 per bad lane.
- Record index jumps to the next session after an answer with both pending pairs: 119 of 237 are +1 and 26 are > 5; after an answer with only one pending pair: 39 of 165 are +1 and 98 jump by more than 5 or backwards. Consistent with subtree jumps, not a proof of loss.
- Scoring of the prediction: 1 held (the same 24 lanes, same sessions); 2 not testable (no first divergence session offline); 3 not testable; 4 held (tmax not the cause); 5 half: 20 of 24 end on an empty answer (predicted all 24: 0.7 missed); 6 not supported (predicted 0.45); 7 refuted (no extra words); 8 held.
## Proposed fix and test (needs approval; not run)
Evidence points away from lane exits and carried words, and toward something that depends on several lanes with different rays being in the same session (identical lanes in R2 were exact). Test, one run, same C1 data: a variant in which only lane 0 of each warp is active (the other 31 lanes leave the kernel before TTUOPEN), 32 warps in one block, so every query runs alone in its warp. If the neighbour sets are then exact, the loss is an interaction between lanes in one session (then the fix is a design change: one active lane per session, or grouping lanes with identical or nested queries, not a code fix). If still inexact, the loss is in the single lane loop (resume inputs) and the next step is to compare single lane sessions with R2's host rounds. Committed prediction: exact for all 32 queries with one lane per warp: 0.6; sessions equal OptiX candidates or +1: 0.55; still inexact: 0.4. A one lane per warp build needs one more check rule (lane id test before the group, no other change), approval for it and for the run.
