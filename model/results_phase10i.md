## Test 1: the sibling DRAM slowdown (F238, F243, F254)
(a) L2 sector hit rate of the stream alone (plain CUDA replica ttu/bench/Conc_5/l2probe.cu, ncu, 6,000 iterations, second launch): 40 MB 1.000, 56 MB 1.000, 72 MB 0.981, 96 MB 0.603, 120 MB 0.488, 512 MB 0.224, 1,024 MB 0.225. Prediction (within 0.10 absolute at every footprint): HELD at 6 of 7 (0.08 at 72, 0.09 at 96, 0.06 at 120, 0.09 at 512), MISSED at 1,024 MB (0.225 against 0.065). The original stream hash does not cover a large footprint uniformly: 512 and 1,024 MB give the same 22 percent hit rate, while a uniform random line choice (the new page version) gives 4.3 percent at 512 MB. So the footprint axis of F254 overstates the DRAM share at 512 and 1,024 MB.
(b) H_miss (RT ratio on the siblings one function of the stream's miss line rate m = lines per second x (1 - hit rate)): pooled Spearman -0.58 over 14 arms (rule: at most -0.9), 11 of 20 pairs with m within 1.5 times differ by more than 0.10 in ratio (rule: none). FAILS (prediction: fails, held). Example: unthrottled 512 MB (m 6.2 G lines/s, r 0.024) against the 150 microsecond sleep arm (m 4.2, r 0.31).
(c) Pages per warp load (new stream type l2fp<MB>p<P>, 32 lanes read lines from P distinct 2 MB pages), siblings, unthrottled, 3 repetitions, alone run before every arm, 0 wrong records. 64 MB (L2 resident, hit rate 1.00): r = 0.203, 0.192, 0.145, 0.121, 0.103, 0.091 for P = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, the stream's own rate 59.8, 57.0, 45.2, 38.3, 32.8, 29.4 G lines/s; the geometric midpoint of r(1) and r(32) is 0.136, first crossed at P = 8. 512 MB (hit rate 0.04): 0.024, 0.023, 0.024, 0.025, 0.024, 0.024 (no P effect). Predictions: r(1) 0.2 to 0.3, r(32) 0.08 to 0.12, knee at P 4 to 16, at 512 MB ratio under 2 and r 0.02 to 0.05: all HELD. SM clock 2,730 MHz with the stream against 2,520 alone (the F244 effect: ratios are not clock corrected).
Reading: two separate effects, not one. (1) Even with every line an L2 hit, spreading one warp load over more pages cuts RT to 0.45 of the one page case with a knee near 8 pages per load. (2) A DRAM sourced stream costs a further factor of about 4 at 512 MB and is independent of the page count. Neither is the DRAM line rate alone. The hardware queue is still not identified.

## Test 2: what limits the warp step knee (F199 to F201)
Checked Unit_1 remap build (t1r, check_seq ALL PASS, stage 0 and the smallest stage 1 launch first), soup 100,000 and 1,000,000 triangles, 32 warps, 30 launches, 3 rotated repetitions, 66 runs, no fault, SM clock median 2,520 MHz (max 2,700). Rays that hit nothing of one class (12/4/9 and 15/5/10 nodes, slots, triangles). far = D distinct rays of the class; near = D rays that are the same ray moved by multiples of 1e-6 (different values, same counts by the exact replay, checked for all 32); identical = the floor. Cycles per ray per SM:
size 100,000, floor 18.0: far D = 2 4 8 16 32: 15.8 19.8 49.3 76.6 84.9; near: 18.0 17.9 18.1 18.0 18.0.
size 1,000,000, floor 20.5: far: 21.5 25.9 77.3 80.1 83.8; near: 20.4 20.2 20.2 20.4 20.3.
x(D) = (near - floor) / (far - floor) is 0.00 at D 16 and 32 at both sizes (rule: H_rays at least 0.75, H_lines at most 0.25): H_lines. Prediction: mixed 50 percent, H_lines 30 percent: MISSED (the less likely outcome). Far reproduces F199 within 15 percent (85 and 84 against 86 and 87). So the unit's cost follows the number of distinct node paths (lines) in a warp, not the number of distinct ray values; with F201 (the same rays, shifted lanes, cost doubles) this says the merge key is the node request, not the ray. The knee is between 4 and 8 distinct paths per warp at both sizes (far cost jumps 2 to 3 times from D 4 to 8). Not tested: rays that hit, near rays whose paths differ in only some steps, other warp counts, one chip.

## Test 3: short query sibling pair on the 4090 (prepared, stage 0 only)
build_patch.py now accepts --outer with --stride 0 --same-record (every round of a lane reads and writes its own record, so the output stays small; the only change in the instruction stream is an immediate). Build: --slim --remap --compact-out --regs 96 --max-out 61 --loop 32 --outer 1000 --stride 0 --same-record --hoist --coal (stage 0 sha256 a0e2870d, stage 1 3d20d76f), check_seq ALL PASS for both, stage 0 run at G 1 and G 128 with 13 warps, tri1, coherent rays: exit 0, no error, all blocks ran. The host needs no --outer for this build (the ray file holds one record per lane). Stage 1 NOT run: new sequence type, awaiting approval. Why the first attempt failed (Phase 10g): the old loop build writes a 56 byte record per ray (memory bound, 45 rays/us at G 1) and the host limits a ray file to 2^24 rays (G 128 with R 200 is 22.9 M). Prediction when approved: one SM at least 0.06 rays per cycle (else void), G 64 at least 60 times one SM, G 128 over G 64 1.3 to 1.6 (60 percent), all blocks overlapping.
