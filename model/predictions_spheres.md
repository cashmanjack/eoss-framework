# Built in spheres against custom primitive spheres against triangles: predictions (2026-11-17, tethys RTX 4090 GPU 1, written BEFORE any Sphere_1 timing was read; OptiX only, no TTU code of ours)

Question (Paper 1 characterization, Paper 2 sphere to triangle baseline, Feature_8): what does one candidate sphere cost when the spheres are (S) OptiX built in spheres, (C) custom primitives (box plus our own ray sphere intersection program), (T0) inscribed icosphere triangles (20 per sphere) or (T1) one more subdivision (80 per sphere), on the same point sets, at one warp and at saturation? And does the built in sphere test run in the RT core or on the SM (the Feature_8 hypothesis: on the SM on Ada)?

Program: ttu/bench/Sphere_1 (sphere.cu, sphere_dev.cu, sphere_params.h, run.py). Closest hit, tmax 1e16, geometry flag disable any hit, 4 payload words, one hit group, trace depth 1. The custom intersection program reads the centre from global memory (one float3, L2 after the first touch), computes the nearer root and reports it. The sphere index of a hit is primitive index / triangles per sphere.

Two designs (one point set per design, the same for all four arms, centres identical).
- D1, controlled candidates (like F49 and F67): N spheres of radius 0.05 along z, each centre 0.9 r off the z axis in x and y (signs cycle through four corners), z spread over [-0.9, 0.9]. Rays go along +z, origin (jitter, jitter, -2) with jitter 0.05 r around the axis. Every sphere's box contains the ray and no sphere is hit (distance of the axis to a centre 1.27 r, jitter at most 0.07 r), so candidates per ray = N exactly and no closest hit program runs. N = 1, 4, 16, 64, 256. One warp: every lane the same ray, 12 launches, the first dropped, median of the lane median cycles around optixTrace. Saturation: 4,194,304 rays (hash of the index, jittered), cycles per ray per SM = kernel time x measured SM clock x 128 SMs / rays (clock from clock64 over globaltimer in calibration launches, as Ovh_1). Per candidate cost b = slope of a least squares line over N (intercept a is the fixed cost of one query).
- D2, random rays on a random point set: 20,000 centres uniform in [-1,1]^3, r = 0.02, 0.04, 0.08, 1,048,576 rays for the per ray results (hit, t, sphere), 4,194,304 rays for saturation (random origin in the cube, random direction, as Ovh_1). Candidates per ray c_IS = intersection program calls of the custom arm (a counting build of the same program). For D2 only per ray cost and the ratio to the custom arm are reported, not a slope.

Units: cycles. Candidate = one sphere whose box the ray enters before it leaves the scene (D1: all N).

Predictions (reasons in brackets, from F49, F67, F180, F186, F194):
1. Correctness. D1: zero hits in every arm. D2: custom and built in sphere agree on hit flag and sphere index for at least 99.9 percent of rays and t within 1e-4 relative; tri arms report a hit for 0.85 to 0.95 (T0) and 0.95 to 0.99 (T1) of the rays the analytic arms hit (inscribed tessellation loses the rim: the icosahedron's mean projected area is 0.76 of the sphere's, 0.93 for 80 faces, but a ray that loses the first sphere can hit a later one); when both hit the same sphere the median |dt| / r is 0.03 to 0.10 (T0) and 0.01 to 0.03 (T1).
2. Counting. D1: the counting custom arm reports exactly N calls per ray. D2: mean calls per ray c_IS about 2 to 5 and nearly flat in r (closest hit shrinks tmax; every second box crossed holds a hit sphere), within 40 percent across the three radii.
3. One warp, D1, slope b over N (cycles per candidate, identical lanes, L2 warm, L1 cold): C 1,000 to 1,600 (central 1,300: F49's 950 to 1,020 for an empty program plus the centre load, one L2 round trip about 290); S 700 to 1,400 (central 1,000: the same kind of SM round trip, no SBT dispatch but also a data fetch); T0 400 to 1,100 (central 800: about 1.5 nodes at 343, 1.5 leaves at 113 and 3 triangles at 52 per candidate sphere, F67); T1 600 to 1,600 (central 1,150: one more level). So at one warp the three kinds are within a factor 2 of each other and C is the largest; C / T0 between 1.1 and 3.
4. One warp intercepts a (N extrapolated to 0): 3,000 to 5,500 cycles for all four arms (F186: first trace call 4,988 including OptiX overhead), within 25 percent of each other.
5. Saturation, D1, slope b (cycles per candidate per ray per SM): C 8 to 35 (central 15: F180 about 450 cycles of throughput per warp per call, all 32 lanes call together here, 450 / 32 = 14); S 6 to 30 (central 12); T0 4 to 14 (central 7: F194, 1.3 per node, 0.67 per triangle, about 3 nodes and 5 triangles per candidate); T1 5 to 20 (central 10). Ratios: C / S between 1.0 and 2.5; C / T0 between 1.2 and 6 (central 2.1): the per candidate gap between a custom primitive and a triangle is much smaller at saturation than at one warp's 8 times (F49) because the calls of 32 converged lanes share one warp call.
6. Saturation, D1 intercept a (N = 0): 8 to 20 cycles per ray per SM for all arms (Ovh_1 / F186: 14.4 for one triangle), the arms within 40 percent of each other.
7. D2 saturation, cycles per ray per SM: ordering T0 <= S <= C at every radius (T arms cheapest when c_IS is 2 to 5), S / C between 0.5 and 1.0, T0 / C between 0.2 and 1.0. The cost per ray changes by less than 2 times from r = 0.02 to 0.08 for every arm.
8. Where the built in sphere runs: ncu on the D1 N = 64 saturation launch (warp instructions executed per warp per candidate, from smsp__inst_executed): C 100 to 400, S 80 to 350, T0 and T1 under 15. Reading rule written now: S is SM resident (the Feature_8 hypothesis holds) if its warp instructions per candidate are above 50 AND its saturated slope b is above 0.4 times C's; S is in the RT core if instructions per candidate are under 15; anything else is reported as not determined. If ncu is not allowed on tethys this item is not answered and the slope ratio alone is reported as supporting evidence only.
9. Memory: GAS bytes S and C within a factor 2 of each other (1 to 3 MB for 20,000 spheres); T0 20 to 40 MB, T1 80 to 160 MB for D2 (F67: about 65 B per triangle).

Reading rules (written now):
- "Held" for a range = measured inside it. Central values are scored as relative error. A slope is only reported when the least squares line over the five N has R^2 above 0.98; otherwise the per N values are reported and no slope.
- The sphere to triangle baseline (Paper 2) is the pair (b_C, b_T0, b_T1) at one warp and at saturation, with the accuracy of item 1. If T0 is cheaper than C at saturation by less than 1.2 times, tessellation does not pay on this GPU at that accuracy and that is the finding.
- One run per cell, 10 launches (saturation) and 12 launches (one warp), no cell repeated after seeing it, except a failed run (reported).

## Addendum 2026-11-17 (after D1 and D2 were run and read, before D1b and ncu): D1b predictions

What happened in D1 (read before this addendum; numbers in model/results_spheres.md): with the axis 1.27 r from every centre the triangle arms did nothing per sphere (slope 3.4 and 88 cycles per sphere at one warp, R^2 0.87 and 0.83, against my 400 to 1,600): the axis ray crosses no triangle box, because the tree of the tessellation is built over triangles, not spheres, and an inscribed face's box does not reach the axis. My predictions 3 and 5 for T0 and T1 assumed a ray that visits a subtree per candidate sphere; that was wrong for this geometry, so D1 does not test the triangle arms. Only S and C (whose box is the sphere's box and does contain the axis) were measured meaningfully there.

D1b: the same program, the near miss line: every centre 0.72 r off the axis in x and y (axis at 1.018 r from the centre, so every sphere box still holds the ray and no sphere is hit), ray jitter 0.005 r (nearest approach at least 1.011 r, zero hits expected in every arm). The tri arms now have the surface 0.018 r from the axis, so the faces near that point (and their boxes) are crossed. S and C are unchanged geometry (same boxes crossed): expected within 10 percent of D1 for both the slope and the intercept at one warp and at saturation.
Predictions for D1b:
10. One warp slope b (cycles per candidate sphere): T0 150 to 700 (central 350: one leaf visit and 1 to 3 triangle tests per sphere, about one L2 round trip), T1 150 to 800 (central 400). R^2 over N at least 0.95 for both.
11. Saturation slope b: T0 0.5 to 4 (central 1.5), T1 0.5 to 5 (central 2). So the tessellated sphere stays at most about as expensive per candidate as S and C (D1: 3.0 to 3.5) and probably cheaper by 1.5 to 6 times; if T0 and T1 are both within 20 percent of C, or above it, the Paper 2 expectation (triangles cheaper per candidate) fails on this GPU in the near miss regime.
12. Triangles tested per sphere is not measured (no counter); the reading of 10 and 11 does not depend on it.
13. ncu (smsp__inst_executed.sum, saturation launch of 1,048,576 rays, D1 N = 16, 64, 256, difference over N divided by warps and by the extra spheres): C 100 to 400 warp instructions per candidate (the original item 8) is withdrawn and replaced, because the D1 saturation slope (3.0 cycles per candidate per ray per SM = 96 SM cycles per warp call, 4 issue slots per cycle would allow about 380 instructions) fixes the upper end: C 40 to 380, S 40 to 380, T0 and T1 under 15 as before. The decision rule of item 8 stands unchanged (above 50 and slope ratio above 0.4: SM resident; under 15: unit; else not determined).
