## Test 1: what makes the sibling DRAM slowdown (F238, F243, F254)
Three parts, all plain CUDA streams beside our checked TTU kernel (random rays on the siblings), except (a).
(a) Stream L2 hit rate, alone, by ncu (plain CUDA replica of the l2fp stream, finite run, no TTU, no OptiX): lts__t_sector_hit_rate and dram__bytes_read at footprints 40, 56, 72, 96, 120, 512, 1024 MB. Prediction: hit rate about min(0.95, 66 MB / footprint): 0.95 / 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.69 / 0.55 / 0.13 / 0.065; accepted within 0.10 absolute at each footprint (probability 60 percent).
(b) Hypothesis H_miss: the RT ratio on the siblings is ONE decreasing function of the stream's miss line rate m = (lines per second of the arm, F254) x (1 - measured hit rate from (a)), over the 14 sib arms of F254 (7 footprints, unthrottled and 150 microsecond sleep, existing data). Rule fixed now: HOLDS if pooled Spearman(r, m) <= -0.9 and every pair of arms with m within 1.5 times has r within 0.10. Prediction: it FAILS (75 percent): the throttled 512 MB arm (about 4 billion missing lines per second, r 0.31) against the unthrottled 120 MB arm (comparable m, r 0.037) will not match, because what an RT load waits behind is the queue the stream builds, not the DRAM rate alone.
(c) Pages per warp load: new stream type l2fp<MB>p<P> (plain CUDA): the 32 lanes of a warp load read 32 random lines taken from P distinct 2 MB pages (32 / P lines per page), footprint 64 MB (about L2 sized) and 512 MB (DRAM), P = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, sib placement, unthrottled, 3 repetitions, alone run before every arm. Predictions: at 64 MB r falls with P from 0.2 to 0.3 (P 1) to 0.08 to 0.12 (P 32), a knee at P between 4 and 16 (the P where r crosses the geometric midpoint of r(1) and r(32)); at 512 MB the P effect is weak (r(1) / r(32) < 2, all r between 0.02 and 0.05). Probability of both 55 percent. F243's 2 pages 0.19 and 32 pages 0.076 are the anchors.
What each outcome means: (b) fails and (c) shows a knee: the loss is set by how many pages and misses one warp load keeps outstanding (a per warp, per page resource), not by DRAM traffic. (b) holds: the DRAM miss rate is the cause and the page effect is a side effect.

## Test 2: what limits the warp step knee (F199 to F201)
Question: does the unit's cost depend on distinct rays in a warp or on distinct node lines per step? New ray files only (data, no new sequence): the checked Unit_1 remap build (t1r), soup 100,000 and 1,000,000 triangles, 32 warps, 30 launches, rays that hit nothing, the class of F199 design B. Per size, D = 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 distinct rays per warp (every warp the same 32 lanes, 32 / D lanes per ray):
 far: D rays of the class (distinct paths), the F199 cls<g> files with g = 32 / D;
 near: D rays that are tiny perturbations (origin moved by multiples of 1e-6 across the direction) of ONE class ray, so they have different ray values but the same path; counts checked equal by the exact replay (precondition, otherwise the perturbation is reduced; arms with unequal counts are not scored);
 identical: the same ray in all lanes (the floor, g = 32).
x(D) = (c_near(D) - c_floor) / (c_far(D) - c_floor), c = cycles per ray per SM. Rule: H_rays (cost depends on distinct ray values) if x >= 0.75 at D = 16 and 32 at both sizes; H_lines (cost depends on distinct node lines) if x <= 0.25 at both; otherwise mixed. Prediction: MIXED (50 percent), H_lines 30 percent, H_rays 20 percent (F201 found both matter at one mean of 7.1 lines per step). Also predicted: c_far reproduces F199 within 15 percent.
