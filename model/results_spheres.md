Raw runs: d1 (20261117T124609), d2 (T124832), d1b (T125028), ncu2 and ncu3 (ncu: the first ncu directory, notes/sphere1/ncu, profiled an OptiX build kernel for the built in sphere because the kernel filter was missing; discarded, redone with -k optixLaunch). ## What was measured
Four representations of the same sphere set: S built in sphere, C custom primitive (box plus our ray sphere program, centre read from global memory), T0 and T1 inscribed icosphere triangles (20 and 80 per sphere). Closest hit. D1: N spheres along z, axis rays that cross every box. D1b: the same with the axis at 1.018 r from every centre (instead of 1.27 r). D2: 20,000 random centres, random rays, r = 0.02, 0.04, 0.08.

## What this says
1. The built in sphere runs on the SM on Ada, not in the RT core: about 220 to 245 warp instructions per candidate sphere, the same as our custom program (190 to 230), and the same time per candidate (one warp 1,310 to 1,340 cycles against 1,180 to 1,230; saturation 3.1 to 3.5 against 2.8 to 3.0). The Feature_8 hypothesis holds (read from instruction counts, not yet from the OptiX code). The built in sphere is not faster than a custom primitive on this GPU: 0 to 15 percent slower per candidate in D1 and D1b, and in D2 (a real workload) 3 to 16 percent faster at saturation (S / C 0.97, 0.95, 0.84; the custom arm also reads the centre from memory).
2. A candidate sphere costs about 1,200 to 1,340 cycles of latency at one warp (about 4 to 5 L2 round trips worth) but only about 3 cycles per ray per SM at saturation when the 32 lanes call together (96 SM cycles per warp call; about 220 instructions per call at 4 issue slots per cycle is 55 cycles of issue). F49's 8 times between a custom and a triangle candidate is a one warp latency number; at saturation the cost depends on what a candidate means for the triangle arm (next item).
3. "Cost per candidate" is not the same thing for spheres and for triangles. A custom or built in sphere is a box in the tree: every ray crossing the box pays the call, hit or not. A tessellated sphere has no sphere level box; the tree is over triangles. In D1 (axis 1.27 r away) the triangle arms were nearly free (0.04 and 0.38 cycles per sphere at saturation) because no triangle box reached the axis; in D1b (axis 1.018 r away, 0.018 r outside the sphere) they cost 4.5 (T0) and 7.2 (T1) cycles per sphere at saturation, MORE than the 2.8 and 3.1 of C and S, and 540 and 850 cycles per sphere at one warp, half to two thirds of C and S. So the sphere to triangle gap per candidate is not a constant: it ranges from 80 times cheaper (far miss) to 1.6 to 2.6 times dearer (near miss at saturation), set by how many triangle boxes the ray reaches.
4. On a realistic workload (D2, random rays on 20,000 spheres) tessellation does not pay: T0 costs 0.98, 1.07, 1.07 times C at saturation, T1 1.16, 1.15, 0.95, and S 0.97, 0.95, 0.84 times C; T0 loses 7 percent of the ray hits at r = 0.02 (7 percent of the rays that hit analytic spheres are missed; the median hit distance is off by 0.2 r for T0 and 0.06 r for T1, and at r = 0.08 only 14 percent (T0) and 45 percent (T1) of the first hits are on the same sphere as the analytic result, because neighbours overlap). Whole ray cost is dominated by traversal of incoherent rays (84 to 280 cycles per ray per SM, F182 order), not by the candidate tests. The Paper 2 baseline on this GPU is therefore: sphere to triangle at 20 and 80 triangles per sphere gives no speedup over custom or built in spheres for random rays, at a cost in accuracy and 25 to 100 times the BVH memory.

## Caveats
- One run per cell; the D1 one warp curves have 5 points; T0 and T1 slopes in D1b have R^2 0.93 and 0.97 (the fit is over the number of spheres, which also changes the tree depth).
- The saturation cost per sphere comes from lanes that all call together (identical rays); lanes that diverge in the tree call separately and the cost per ray is higher (D2: about 25 to 30 cycles per extra call per ray is implied by D2 against D1, not measured separately).
- "Candidate" counted by construction in D1, by intersection program calls in D2 (custom only). Triangle tests per sphere were not counted (no counter).
- The residency of the built in sphere is inferred from instruction counts and times, not from the OptiX code; reading the code first (Feature_8's first step) is still open.
- The tessellations are inscribed (vertices on the sphere); circumscribed or area matched ones would trade accuracy differently. Only N = 20,000 and one radius per design; BVH sizes below the L2.
