# Treelet_3 predictions and rule, tethys (RTX 4090 GPU 1), 2026-11-24. Written BEFORE any arm was timed at scale. OptiX only (no TTU code of ours is run), no process matching by name, notes/paper untouched.

Task (Jack): the most aggressive treelet design we can build, measured end to end against the better of natural order and Morton; rule: at least 1.2 times.

## What was built (ttu/bench/Treelet_3: treelet3.cu, treelet3_dev.cu, treelet3_old_dev.cu, treelet3_params.h, run3.py)
1. Hardware inspector: an IAS with one instance per treelet (2,744 on the soup, 1,118 on Rungholt). The instances' children are a shared 12 triangle unit cube GAS scaled to the treelet's tight box (shell proxy), NOT the 121 KB treelet GASes themselves: tracing the real GASes would do the full intersection work, so only a proxy can give "which treelets, in what order" from the hardware. A trace against it (closest hit, back faces culled, an any hit program that drops events at or before the cursor and entry faces behind the origin of boxes that do not contain it; origin pulled back by the largest box diagonal so that the entry face of a box containing the origin is a front hit) returns the next treelet along the ray after a cursor (entry distance, id), up to a distance bound. Walking it with the bound = best hit so far gives exactly the treelets whose box the ray enters before its closest hit, in order of entry: complete by construction (no verification trace, no fallback). A self test at start checks the winding (entering ray found, ray from inside not).
2. Fused design: A1 (one launch, natural order, hardware inspector: first treelet of every ray, cursor, histogram); scan (one block: counts, queue bases with capacity 3 x count + 1024, TPC ranges balanced by rays) and counting enqueue of 4 byte ray indices (atomic tail per treelet queue); then ONE persistent launch: pinned warps (TPC = %smid / 2, 64 TPCs, WPS warps per SM) probe their TPC's treelets from a per TPC pointer, claim up to 32 entries with one CAS, trace the rays in the treelet's GAS with tmax = best hit so far, take the next treelet from the inspector, and atomically requeue the 4 byte index into that treelet's queue; steal from other TPCs when the own range is empty; a counter of unfinished rays ends the kernel. No sort of any kind in the drain; the only grouping is the histogram + counting enqueue of the first treelet. Arms f_pin (pinned) and f_nopin (warp id instead of smid, ablation). Everything on the GPU inside the timed span (memsets, reset, all launches).
3. Amortized: f_rep:R = one f_pin pass that records every ray's visited treelets (visit k, up to 16), then the grouping is built once (per level k: radix sort of rays by treelet, gather of the rays into level lists) and R - 1 replays run level by level (persistent pinned warps over contiguous per TPC ranges, no inspector, no queues, no atomics except the chunk counter). Baselines at the same R: nat_rep:R (R natural launches), mo_rep:R (Morton sort and gather once, R x (launch + scatter)). Total time for the R traces, reported per trace.
4. Also ias_nat (natural order through the IAS of the real treelet GASes, to show the two level cost) and e_opT (oracle key, free, pinned: a lower bound for any key based scheme) for context. Baselines nat_R, e_mo5, e_mo4 as in Treelet_2.

## Sets, settings
Soup: 8,000,000 triangles, scene seed 99, grid 14 (2,744 treelets, median 120,704 B); f_rand (7,942,464 hit rays, as in Treelet_2) and f_b3 (7,278,848 bounce rays); rerun of the data generation gives the same sets (same seeds). Rungholt: 6,704,264 triangles, 1,118 treelets of 6,000 triangles (Morton order), camera as Treelet_2 (5,730,048 depth 3 bounce rays, pixel order). WPS 16 and 8, 10 rounds, rotated arm order, 3 processes per cell, median over processes. R = 10 and 100 (fewer rounds). Every result of every arm is checked against the full GAS closest hit (ties with the same t and another triangle counted separately as in Treelet_2).

## Rule (fixed now)
PRIMARY: single pass end to end time of f_pin at WPS 16 is at least 1.2 times faster than the better of nat_R and e_mo5 (e_mo4 also considered: take the best of the three) on BOTH soup sets. SECONDARY (reported, not the verdict): the same at WPS 8; amortized f_rep at R = 10 and R = 100 against the better of nat_rep and mo_rep at the same R; Rungholt (reported by itself). Correctness: 0 wrong rays and 0 t mismatches, queue overflow 0 and spin timeouts 0, otherwise the run is invalid and not used. The verdict is "design meets the rule" only if the primary rule holds with the median over the 3 processes and the worst process is at least 1.1.

## Predictions (numbers are central, range in brackets; ms are 8 M rays, soup random, WPS 16, unless said)
P1 Correctness: 0 wrong rays and 0 t mismatches in all arms (ties only a few on the soup, many on Rungholt as in Treelet_2); overflow 0; the oracle treelet is among the visited treelets for 100 percent of the rays (exactness of the inspector walk).
P2 Visits: mean treelets visited per ray 3.0 (2.0 to 5.0); share of rays whose first visited treelet holds the closest hit 0.5 (0.3 to 0.8); maximum visits 15 to 30.
P3 The IAS of real treelet GASes (ias_nat) is slower than the single GAS natural launch: 1.0 to 1.3 times its time.
P4 Stage times of f_pin, soup random: A1 reset + inspector first step 2.0 (1.0 to 4.0); scan + enqueue 0.6 (0.3 to 1.2); drain 7 (4 to 12); total 9.5 (6 to 16) ms. Natural 11.8 and Morton5 5.78 (Treelet_2 values) should reproduce within 10 percent.
P5 f_pin / best baseline (Morton5) : 0.6 (0.35 to 1.0): the rule is NOT met on the primary test (probability of meeting it 5 percent). f_pin is faster than natural: 1.2 (0.7 to 2.0).
P6 WPS 16 over WPS 8 for f_pin: 1.15 (1.0 to 1.4). f_nopin slower than f_pin by 1.15 (1.0 to 1.5); this is the pinning effect inside the fused design, smaller than the 1.3 of the presorted case because the inspector and queue traffic dilute it.
P7 Share of warp cycles in the drain kernel (clock64, summed over warps): inspector step 35 percent (25 to 50), treelet trace + state loads 30 (20 to 45), claim / probe / entry wait 10 (5 to 20), requeue (cursor write, fence, tail atomic) 8 (3 to 15), idle (nothing to claim) 15 (5 to 35). Queue plus atomic overhead (probe, claim, requeue) 18 percent (8 to 35) of warp time. Tail atomics: about visits minus rays (about 2 per ray).
P8 L1 hit rate of the RT unit in the drain kernel (ncu, same method as Treelet_1): 70 percent (50 to 85) for the treelet traces, i.e. below the presorted pinned 91 because the inspector traces share the L1.
P9 Amortized soup random, WPS 16, per trace: replay with levels, one rep 3.0 ms (2.0 to 5.0); Morton rep (trace sorted + scatter) 2.8 (2.0 to 3.5); natural 11.8. f_rep at R = 10: total / R = 2.8 x 1 + build ... about 6.5 (4 to 10) per trace, below Morton R = 10 (3.1): ratio to the best baseline 0.5. At R = 100: about 3.4 (2.3 to 5.5) per trace against Morton 2.8: ratio 0.85 (0.5 to 1.3). Probability that f_rep meets 1.2 times over Morton at R = 100: 15 percent.
P10 Rungholt: best baseline is natural (1.0 ms). f_pin end to end 5 (2 to 15) ms: ratio 0.2 (0.07 to 0.5); f_rep at R = 100: 1.2 (0.8 to 2.0) ms per trace: ratio 0.8 (0.5 to 1.2), rule not met. The overlapping treelet boxes of Rungholt (big triangles) give many visits (mean 6, 3 to 15).
P11 Verdict (my expectation): the design as built does not meet the 1.2 times rule end to end in a single pass on the soup; it beats natural order clearly (about 1.2 to 2 times) but not Morton; amortization closes part of the gap only at R = 100.

## Method notes
Smoke runs (200,000 triangle soup, grid 6 and 8 treelets per TPC) were run before this file to debug the kernels (hangs: a persistent warp that lets its lanes drift apart deadlocks inside optixTrace, fixed by straight line per chunk code, dummy zero length rays for idle lanes, group based claims); only correctness fields were read from them, no times are used. Timing arms at 8 M and on Rungholt were NOT run before this file.
