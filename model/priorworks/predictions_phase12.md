# Predictions, Phase 12 (2026-11-18, triton GPU 1, OptiX and plain CUDA only, no TTU code). Written before any run of these items.

## A. One timing scope for the F245 table
Scope for every method: data already on the device, index or BVH build plus search, no host set up, no result copy. Host time is a separate column (everything else the method does on the host or the copy of the result back).
Read from the sources first (not measured): CLOVER's span (hubs, warpwise) starts after the point and query copies and ends after a device synchronize, results left on the device, so it already has this scope. RTNN's three timers (query sort, create and upload geometry, search compute) are device side and leave out the result copy, so they have it too; its host part is "total search time" minus those three (mostly the D2H copy). TrueKNN's sample times the whole round loop on the host, including a managed buffer scan on the host after every round and the launch synchronizations, so it does not: I rebuild a copy with timers (build, launches, refits on the device scope; host scan separate), results unchanged.
1. hubs, warpwise and RTNN device numbers stay as in F245 (no rerun of them, only the host column is new; hubs and warpwise host columns need a rebuilt bench with a host wall timer). Probability 95 percent.
2. TrueKNN device scope is below its printed total in all 24 cells; the host part is 5 to 40 percent of the total on the small sets (bunny, 3DRoad) and under 10 percent on dragon. Probability 70 percent.
3. At most 2 of the 24 "fastest" labels change when TrueKNN is re scoped (candidates: uniform 1M K 5, Glove 3D K 5, Manuscript K 5, where TrueKNN was close). Probability 70 percent.
4. RTNN host part (total minus device) is larger than its device scope on at least the Porto, Gowalla, Glove 3D and dragon cells at K 128 (the result copy is N x K x 4 bytes, 0.5 to 1.8 GB). Probability 75 percent.

## B. Density aware RT, kill test: RTNN's own partitioning (-p 1) in exact mode (-a 0)
Cells: Porto 1M, Gowalla, Glove 3D at K 50 and 128 (six cells), the same r99 radius as F245 as the largest radius, query sort off as in F245 (-s 0), everything else as F245, device scope as in A (sort and partition + geometry + search compute; all batches summed). Hubs times from F245.
1. -p 1 -a 0 completes on all six cells (no error, no out of memory). Probability 70 percent.
2. Exactness: with the F245 checker (coincident points excluded) the share exact is at least as high as without partitioning on every cell (partitioning only shrinks the per query radius to a circumscribed ball that still holds K points). Probability 70 percent. Short lists (the 99 percent radius effect) fall to under 0.1 percent. Probability 50 percent.
3. Partitioning lowers RTNN time on every one of the six cells by a factor of 1.5 to 10 (ball sizes follow density). Probability 65 percent.
4. Partitioning flips at most one of the six cells from hubs to RTNN (Porto K 50 is the most likely: RTNN 827 against hubs 96 ms needs 8.6 times). Probability 75 percent. Flips none: 45 percent. Flips three or more: 5 percent.

## C. Optimized RT, kill test: the known callback fixes on RTNN knn mode and TrueKNN
Reading the sources first: both programs already compute the distance in float (RTNN `float sqdist`, TrueKNN `std::sqrt` of floats), so the F151 double to float fix has nothing to fix here. F138's register heap kept 16 entries in registers (K 16); K 50 and 128 need 100 and 256 registers for keys and indices and do not fit (255 register limit, and an unrolled insertion of that size). So the heap fix can be tested honestly only at K 5 (RTNN: 5 keys and 5 indices carried in payload registers instead of a local array behind a pointer; TrueKNN: the 5 neighbour records loaded, inserted in registers, written back). For K 50 and 128 the question "could any callback fix flip a cell" is bounded instead with RTNN's stub build (callback insertion removed, rt_stub): if RTNN with a free callback still loses to hubs, no callback fix flips it.
1. RTNN and TrueKNN K 5 variants return bit identical neighbour lists to the unfixed builds (same radii, same rounds). Probability 85 percent.
2. K 5 register heap speeds RTNN's search compute by 1.1 to 1.6 times and TrueKNN's total by 1.0 to 1.3 times (their callbacks are not the dominant cost at K 5 on most sets). Probability 60 percent.
3. Cells that move from hubs to RT with the K 5 fix: at most 1 of the 8 K 5 cells (Glove 3D or 3DRoad). Probability 75 percent.
4. Stub bound: RTNN with a free callback (stub, search compute only) is still slower than hubs on Porto, Gowalla and Glove 3D at K 50 and 128 (all six cells). Probability 80 percent. It beats hubs on 3DRoad and bunny at K 50, 128 (callback is what loses there). Probability 40 percent.
5. So the number of cells that move from hubs to RT with all applicable fixes in total (K 5 real fix, K 50 and 128 stub bound as an upper limit) is between 0 and 3 of 24. Probability 70 percent.

## D. Winner prediction with model v1 unchanged and replay counts (labelled not blind: the F245 times are known)
Model v1 as frozen (FORM_combined_v1.md, score_model_v1.py constants: RTNN form with the reference uniform callback cost, so no per dataset callback measurement; chain L = 575 cycles per candidate). Replay counts: per query candidates in the radius cube and the sphere, computed on the CPU with a kd tree on the same points and the F245 r99 radius (replay of the traversal counts, no GPU). RTNN only; TrueKNN is not predicted (its form needs per round probe warp cycles, which I do not measure for 24 cells). Predicted RT time = model v1 launch time; compared with measured search compute (the quantity the model predicts) and, for the winner, with measured hubs time; second variant adds the measured sort and geometry time (not predicted, labelled).
1. Predicted launch time within a factor 2 of the measured search compute on at least 12 of the 24 cells (8 datasets x K 5, 50, 128). Probability 40 percent (F236: 6 of 14 inside a factor 2, RTNN 2 of 4; this is a new data range, K 128 and clustered sets).
2. Model v1 picks the right winner (RTNN against hubs, using measured hubs) on at least 16 of 24 cells (67 percent, the hubs always win rule gets 18 of 24). Probability 40 percent that it beats the always hubs rule (more than 18). Probability 65 percent that it is at least 16.
3. The model's mistakes are mostly over optimism for RT on clustered data (Porto, Gowalla, Glove 3D at K 50 and 128), because v1 has no term for long per lane candidate lists beyond the chain term. Probability 60 percent.

## E. Blind test pipeline (script test on a synthetic set)
The script given a point file: runs the CPU replay, writes the model v1 predictions (RTNN launch time per K, a hubs time prediction is NOT made by v1) to a file, commits it with git (hash printed) and only then runs the timed methods. The synthetic test set: 300,000 points, mixed clusters.
1. The script runs end to end on the synthetic set and the prediction commit precedes the first timed run by timestamp. Probability 85 percent.
2. Model v1's predicted launch time for the synthetic set is within a factor 3 of RTNN's measured search compute at K 50. Probability 55 percent.
