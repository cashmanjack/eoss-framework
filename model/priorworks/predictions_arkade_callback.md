# Arkade: Glove 3D count mismatch and loaded callback loads, predictions (2026-11-17, triton, written before any run of either)

## A. Glove 3D: recorder 2.56 times the replay (F220)
Facts read from the source before the run: exp2/src/s01-knn/hostCode.cpp builds every sphere and every query as vec3f(x, y, 0.0f), so the third column of every file is dropped. The replay (F207, F220) counts in 3D (Chebyshev box over all three columns of the file). Gowalla and Manuscript files have z = 0 in column 3, so for them 2D and 3D counts are equal. Glove 3D has three real columns.
Hypothesis H_A: the whole 2.56 is the dropped third column. CPU only: count the Chebyshev box (radius r from pred.json, the same 10,000 query rows, float32 coordinates) in x, y only.
- P_A1: the 2D count mean over the 10,000 queries divided by the recorder mean (113,154) is within 0.9 to 1.1 (after the same x 1.10 hardware factor used for the replay, 1.1 x 2D / recorder within 0.85 to 1.15).
- P_A2: 3D count mean x 1.10 reproduces F220's replay (44,173) to 1 percent (checks that the CPU side is the same).
- P_A3: per query, the 2D count tracks the recorder (median per query ratio of recorder over 1.10 x 2D within 0.85 to 1.15; Spearman above 0.95).
- If P_A1 fails (outside 0.7 to 1.4) the dropped column is NOT the cause, and the next suspect is repeated intersection program calls per primitive (not tested here).
- Gowalla and Manuscript: 2D equals 3D exactly (ratio 1.000), so their recorder / replay of 0.91 and 0.97 are unchanged.

## B. Do the callback's dependent global loads slow down under load?
Callback = the Arkade intersection program (float build, FLOATVAR 2, device output buffer, NORM 2). Two timed segments inside it, each ended by a compare on the loaded value so clock64 is read after the load completes: (a) the load of data_pts[primID] (dependent on the primitive index the hardware hands over), (b) the scan of the KN = 50 result slots (49 dependent compare loads on the payload, local memory) when distance < r^2.
Design: one build with per query (a) and (b) cycle sums and counts; Manuscript and Gowalla (float build); queries Q = 32, 320, 3,200, 10,000, 40,000, 160,000 (query rows sampled from the data, same seed) so that occupancy runs from one warp to many warps per SM; each Q with and without a pointer chase kernel running beside it (separate stream, random chain over 1 GiB, one chain per thread, 142 x 2 blocks of 128 threads; the chase reports its own cycles per step during and after the launch). Probes as in the earlier builds (CB_BEGIN/CB_END stay on, so the share of the call is measured in the same run). 3 runs each, GPU idle before, timeout 120 s, OptiX plus plain CUDA only (no TTU code).
- P_B1 (a): per call latency at 32 queries 250 to 500 cycles (L2 hit, data 14 to 26 MB fits L2); at 10,000 queries 1.0 to 1.6 times that; at 160,000 queries 1.3 to 2.5 times that; with the chase beside it at least 2 times the no chase value at every Q (the chase evicts the data from L2 and loads DRAM), up to 5 times.
- P_B2 (b): the 49 dependent loads take 800 to 2,000 cycles at 32 queries (L1 hits, about 30 cycles each, 4 to 5 of them with extra latency of the compare); with more warps per SM the payload spills out of L1 (about 12.8 KB per warp), so 160,000 queries is 1.3 to 3 times the 32 query value; the chase changes (b) by less than 20 percent (payload accesses mostly hit L1 and L2, not DRAM).
- P_B3 (share): (a) + (b) are 30 to 60 percent of the cycles of one callback call at 32 queries (the rest is call overhead, distance math, the cand counter and the probes themselves); the growth of (a) + (b) from 32 to 10,000 queries explains less than half of the growth of the whole per call time (F220 says the per call latency of the float version is 2.5 to 3.2 times the model's).
- P_B4: per call time of the whole callback at 10,000 queries is above its 32 query value by 1.3 to 3 times, so loaded latency exists; if it does not grow at all (below 1.1) the loaded callback latency of F133 is not seen here and the "2 warp calls per step" is the whole story.
What can fail: clock64 around a load that the compiler moves or hoists (checked by reading the SASS for the two segments), the probes (a) and (b) lengthening the callback themselves, the chase evicting the data (intended) and also loading the SMs (co residency changes occupancy of the launch; reported by the sms_used and warps per SM lines), the Q = 160,000 launch being much longer than the watchdog allows on a display GPU (triton GPU 1 is headless).
