## (ii) 1 MB of ordinary loads on the TTU's own SM between the two sessions: WORKS, result favours A
Design (after two failed attempts, see the log): one TTU block (smfilter build, 142 blocks, only the one on SM 0 works) and one plain 128 thread role block on the same SM 0 from a separate plain launch (placement checked first with plain CUDA: a grid of 142 blocks lands one block beside the role on SM 0 in 20 of 20 trials; a lone block does not). The role block polls flag1 with relaxed loads (no fence), then (own8) streams 1 MB with ordinary cached loads (about 70 microseconds) or (own9) waits 70 microseconds on the global timer with no loads, then sets flag2. TTU kernel = the validated sibling build, stage 1 after stage 0 and check_seq clean.
Second session cycles (median) and saved fraction (1 minus session over single session):
| scene | single | twice (nothing between) | stream 1 MB | delay 70 us |
| 68 KB | 100,435 | 39,288 (0.61) | 71,763 (0.29) | 46,968 (0.53) |
| 132 KB | 176,460 | 76,134 (0.57) | 128,588 (0.27) | 102,329 (0.42) |
Prediction A: stream about 0.26 / 0.25 (accept 0.15 to 0.38 / 0.14 to 0.36): HELD (0.29 / 0.27). Prediction B: stream within 0.08 of the delay: MISSED (differences 0.24 and 0.15). The delay control alone costs a little (0.53 against 0.61 at 68 KB, 0.42 against 0.57 at 132 KB; at 132 KB below the 0.44 limit I set, so by the pre set rule the comparison is stream against delay, and it still separates). Reading: ordinary loads through the TTU's own L1 destroy about 45 percent of the saving beyond what the same wait does, so node lines are in the L1 of the TTU's own SM (hypothesis A for the own SM). Not shown: that they are in the sibling's L1 as well (that is test (i)); 1 MB through L1 removes everything in L1 (not separated from a smaller stream).
