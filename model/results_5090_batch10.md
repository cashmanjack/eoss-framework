## A. Does any OptiX 8.1 option report a hit for a ray that starts inside a built in sphere? No.
Header and guide search: the sphere build input has only the geometry flags, SBT fields and the radius buffer; the face culling flags (ray CULL_BACK_FACING_TRIANGLES, CULL_FRONT_FACING_TRIANGLES, geometry DISABLE_TRIANGLE_FACE_CULLING) name triangles; OptixBuiltinISOptions has only motion blur, build flags and curve end caps. The programming guide (section 9.6) says "a ray is allowed to start inside the sphere ... the reported (single) hit would be a back face hit".
Test (inside.cu, one sphere of radius 1 at the origin, 14 rays, 56 option variants = 4 geometry flags (NONE, DISABLE_ANYHIT, DISABLE_TRIANGLE_FACE_CULLING, REQUIRE_SINGLE_ANYHIT_CALL) x 7 ray flags (NONE, the two triangle culls, CULL_DISABLED_ANYHIT, CULL_ENFORCED_ANYHIT, DISABLE_ANYHIT, ENFORCE_ANYHIT) x tmin 0 and 1e-4; closest hit and any hit programs both record hits):
- Origin inside (centre, 0.5 on x both directions, 0.99, 0.999, 0.5 on y, off axis 0.3, diagonal directions) or exactly on the surface: no closest hit, no any hit call, in all 56 variants (0 hits of 56 x 11 ray cases).
- Controls (origin outside, entry in front): hit with the exact entry t (0.010000 and 1.000000), front face, hit kind 206, in 48 of 56 variants; the 8 without a hit are the documented any hit culling combinations (CULL_ENFORCED_ANYHIT where the geometry does not enforce any hit: 6; CULL_DISABLED_ANYHIT with DISABLE_ANYHIT geometry: 2). Away from the sphere: miss in all.
So the guide's statement does not hold on this driver and GPU for the built in sphere (it matches F428: entry root only). Not tested: other drivers or GPUs (Ada), a GAS built with PREFER_FAST_TRACE together with matching builtin IS build flags, instance transforms.

## B. Fixed radius search with built in spheres and entry only hits (kill test)
Design (nn_dev.cu): SPH = built in spheres of radius r(1 + 5e-4) around every point, ray from q minus L d to q, d = (1, 0, 0), L = 2 r (1 + 1e-3), the any hit program computes the float distance of the point to q, inserts it into the query's K nearest list if at most r, and calls optixIgnoreIntersection. Why it is exact: if |q - p| is at most r, the origin is at least L - r > r(1 + 5e-4) from p, so it is outside p's sphere, and the line through q enters the sphere before q, so the entry root lies in (0, L]. The origin is never inside a sphere that contains q, which is what the entry only hardware needs. Cost of the design: the callback also runs for every sphere whose entry the segment crosses without containing q (cylinder of radius r and length 2r: 6.28 r^3 against the ball 4.19 r^3).
CUS = RTNN style custom primitives: AABB cube of side 2r(1 + 1e-4) per point, ray from q with tmax 1e-3 r, the intersection program does the same insertion and reports nothing. Both arms share the insertion code (global memory lists). HUBS = CLOVER hubs. RTNN = the real RTNN program (knn mode) on the same radii, built for K 5 and 50 from the existing sm_120 tree.
Data: uniform 1M (F245 recipe) and 50 Gaussian clusters of 20,000 points (sigma 0.02), 1M points, shuffled. Every point is a query (self counts as one of K, hubs convention). r = 0.99 quantile of the K-th nearest distance over 3,000 sampled queries (F245 recipe), so about 1 percent of the lists hold fewer than K points by construction (1.0 to 1.1 percent measured).

### Exactness (against a kd tree, 20,000 sampled queries per cell, both orders of the data; list size, distances equal to the true K smallest within the ball (tolerance 1e-5), no invalid or duplicate ids)
SPH and CUS: 0 wrong in 20,000 sampled queries in all 4 cells (unsorted and Morton sorted runs: 8 checks per arm). ## Reading
1. The entry only sphere search is exact (no missed or extra neighbours in 20,000 sampled queries per cell, equal to the custom primitive arm on all 1M queries up to ties) with a ray that starts at q minus 2r (not minus r: with minus r the origin can lie inside the sphere of a neighbour at distance r and the hardware reports nothing; minus 2r and length 2r is the shortest outside origin that is guaranteed).
2. It does not win. SPH has 0.72 to 0.74 times the candidates of CUS (1.37 to 1.49 balls against 1.86 to 2.08), but each candidate costs more: SPH is slower than CUS in all four cells, 1.19, 1.04, 1.61, 1.10 times in the sorted runs and 1.25, 1.03, 1.38, 1.09 in the unsorted runs. With the insertion code removed (count only, no list) the gap is larger: 1.33, 1.94, 1.98, 1.89 times, i.e. the any hit dispatch plus optixIgnoreIntersection per candidate costs 2.7 to 2.9 times the custom intersection program per candidate (count only: SPH 0.72 x the candidates and 1.9 x the time). The insertion into the lists (global memory, K 50 shifts) is the same code in both arms and hides part of that gap at K 50. The F428 hardware advantage (the built in test runs in the RT core, 0 SM instructions) does not survive here, because the neighbour search needs a callback for every candidate and a built in sphere's callback is an any hit program, which is more expensive than the custom intersection program.
3. Kill criterion (written before the runs): SPH is not at least 1.2 times faster than the faster of CUS and hubs in any of the four cells (it is never faster than CUS; hubs is slower than CUS in three cells and faster in one). The sphere idea is killed as a speedup for this workload on this GPU, with this any hit design.
4. Against the others: on uniform 1M at K 5 my CUS arm takes 2.2 to 2.7 ms against hubs 95 ms and RTNN's 32.5 (0.73 for its search kernel); at K 50 uniform 38 to 42 against hubs 109 and RTNN 38.4 (6.7 search). On the clustered set hubs wins at K 50 (111 against 275 to 330 for CUS, 125 for RTNN with its sort, 94.6 for RTNN's search) and loses at K 5 (94 against 18 to 27). So the RT arms beat hubs at K 5 by 36 to 43 times on uniform data and 3.4 to 5.3 times on the clustered set, and by 2.6 to 2.8 times at K 50 on uniform data (CUS, with the SPH between), in this scope; RTNN's own kernel is 3 to 6 times faster than my CUS (K 5 and K 50, both sets), which says my insertion (lists in global memory) is not the best implementation: absolute times of SPH and CUS are not RTNN's, only their ratio is the result. The 5090 hubs numbers (95 and 109 ms) equal the triton ones (89.6, 105.8; F245), the RT arms are much faster than triton's F245 RTNN totals (37.5 and 46.3 ms) consistent with those totals being dominated by RTNN's own query sort (27 to 30 ms here too) while the 5090 search kernel takes 0.7 to 7 ms on the uniform set (not tested on triton this session).
5. Mean ball counts: K 5 11.5 (uniform), K 50 96 (not the 70 of the Poisson estimate: queries within r of a face of the unit cube have a smaller ball, so the 99 percent radius is set by the boundary), clustered 248 and 1,847 (50 and 37 times K); the cluster cores are about 2,000 times denser than the tails that set the radius, so every RT arm pays hundreds to thousands of candidates per query there while hubs does not.

## Caveats and what went wrong
- CLOVER hubs built natively for sm_120 (nvcc -arch=sm_120, the F227 harness clover_bench.cu) returns wrong lists (all zeros in the first run; 500 of 500 sampled queries wrong, also bitonic; warpwise is right; compute-sanitizer reports no memory error; the cause is not found). The same sources compiled to compute_89 PTX and JIT compiled by the driver return 0 wrong in 1,000 sampled queries in all four cells, and run in 88 to 116 ms, equal to the triton numbers. All hubs times here are from the JIT build; the failed native runs are kept in notes/pc14_nn (hubs_* files, not used).
- Real RTNN: with the default memory setting its auto grid (1.46e9 cells) fails with an invalid address space error on this GPU; with RTNN_DRAM_GB=11.5958 (the setting of the earlier 5090 workload runs) it runs. 3 repetitions per cell, -p 0 -s 0 -sm knn. Its result lists were not checked.
- My insertion code keeps the lists in global memory (no registers), so SPH and CUS absolute times are 3 to 6 times above RTNN's search kernel; the comparison SPH against CUS is like for like, the comparison with hubs and RTNN is only a scale.
- SPH and CUS return only points within r, exact for the 99 percent of queries whose ball holds K points; hubs is exact for all; this is the F245 convention.
- One data draw per set, one GPU, one radius per K, 5 repetitions spread within 1 percent. The SM clock was not logged.
- Mistakes: my first Poisson estimate of the mean ball count ignored the cube boundary (K 50 missed); the first hubs build (native sm_120) produced wrong results and was found by the CLOVER check, not used; the tally sentence above was first written with an invented count and replaced by the statement list.
