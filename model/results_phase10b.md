## 1. Opacity micromaps (Omm_1)

Scene: 100,000 or 1,000,000 random triangles in the unit cube, 8,388,608 random rays, closest hit, alpha pattern = a disc hole in barycentric space around (1/3, 1/3), four radii by primitive id mod 4 (transparent fractions of the triangle 0.141, 0.389, 0.678, 0.846). Micro triangle states classified exactly, so plain any hit is the truth. Variants: A plain any hit (alpha test in the program), A0 the same with the pipeline flag allowOpacityMicromaps 0, D any hit disabled (opaque), D0 flag 0, B(L) 4 state micromap at subdivision L with the same any hit resolving unknown states, C(L) 2 state micromap (unknown = opaque) with any hit disabled.

Measurement design, and a correction. First pass: one process per variant, 2 million rays, 3 repeats: spread across processes 3 to 14 percent (my 3 percent rule failed). Second pass (kept raw next to it): 8 million rays, 5 repeats: still 3 to 14 percent, and the within process spread was only 7 percent of the max minus min over 20 launches. Third design (the one reported): all variants built in one process, launches interleaved round robin with a rotating start, 30 rounds, 8 processes. Within a process the round to round spread is 0.0 to 0.6 percent (IQR), but the ratio of one variant to A changes by 3 to 12 percent from one process to the next (same rays, same code): the memory placement of each process (each variant's GAS and buffers) moves times by up to about 10 percent. So only differences above about 10 percent are resolved; ratios below are medians over 8 processes with the min to max range.

Results (N 1,000,000; N 100,000 in brackets where it differs):
- Correctness: B(L) equals A on hit, primitive and t on every ray, all 8 processes, all four levels (0 differences in 8,388,608 rays). C(L) differs from A on 33.8, 11.6, 3.2, 0.8 percent of rays at L 2, 4, 6, 8 (29.9, 9.8, 2.6, 0.7 at 100,000): the unknown area is the whole error; it falls 3 to 4 times per two levels (about 2 per level, as predicted).
- Any hit invocations per ray: A 1.83 (1.50 at 100,000). B(2) 1.00, B(4) 0.263, B(6) 0.066, B(8) 0.017: as a fraction of A 0.548, 0.144, 0.036, 0.009 (identical at both sizes). The hardware resolves transparent and opaque micro triangles without the program: it is an Ada hardware path, not software.
- Time over A (median, range over processes): B(2) 1.04 (0.96 to 1.07), B(4) 1.04 (0.94 to 1.06), B(6) 1.05 (0.96 to 1.06), B(8) 1.10 (1.07 to 1.14). So micromaps with the unknown states resolved by an any hit program do NOT beat plain any hit here (100,000: 1.02 to 1.10). C(2) 0.78, C(4) 0.95, C(6) 1.03, C(8) 1.12 of A (C(6) is as slow as A with no any hit invocation at all).
- Opaque D is 0.63 of A (0.66 at 100,000): the plain alpha test costs about 1.6 times opaque. The flag allowOpacityMicromaps alone: A0 over A 0.99 to 1.03 (inside the noise).
- GAS size: +23 percent with micromaps (6.51 to 8.03 MB, 65.1 to 80.3 MB), the micromap array itself is 0.64 KB (L2) to 66 KB (L8).
- ncu (one launch of 2,097,152 rays, N 1,000,000): SM warp instructions A 162 M, B(4) 80 M, B(6) 63 M, C(6) 43.4 M, D 43.2 M; sectors at L1 A 357 M, B(4) 369 M, B(6) 377 M, C(6) 365 M, D 233 M. So C(6) removes the SM work entirely (same warp instructions as D) but its memory traffic equals A's: the micromap lookup costs the unit about what the any hit costs the SM. For B the instructions above D fall less than the invocation count (31 percent of the extra at B(4) with 14 percent of the invocations) because one remaining invocation in a warp still runs for the whole warp.

Scoring of my predictions (predictions_phase10b.md section 2): held: exact equality of B with A; invocation ratios B(2), B(4), B(6) (0.5 to 0.9, 0.12 to 0.3, 0.03 to 0.09); A over D at 100,000 (1.2 to 2.5: 1.52); hardware path. Missed: time over A for every B(L) (I said 0.6 to 0.95, 0.35 to 0.65, 0.25 to 0.5; measured 1.04 to 1.05); C(6) over D 1.0 to 1.5 (1.65); C(L) error 1 to 10 percent at L 2 (33.8); any hit invocations 8 to 25 per ray at N 1,000,000 (1.83: closest hit with an any hit shrinks the ray quickly); A over D 3 to 8 at 1,000,000 (1.59); GAS growth under 10 percent (23); the SM instruction count rule (within 20 percent of the invocation ratio: not). Disclosed before the timing runs: the smoke numbers (invocation ratio 0.14 at L4, +23 percent GAS).

## 4. Transform sequence, prepare only (Xform_1)

Plan: model/plan_xform1.md. Stage 1 was not run and its binary was not run by anything (stage 0 binaries only).
- setup3 --imat (id, rz37, rz90s2, rx37, snu; default id keeps every earlier run unchanged). ias_words.py from the private IAS dump gives the same eight store words as before; the IAS stage 0 and stage 1 builds pass check_seq ALL PASS (A1 to A13, B12 to B15: 8 stores, GO, 6 loads, fuse count 15, close bit on the last load only, even register pairs, no branch inside the group).
- Stage 0 (no TTU instruction), one warp: OptiX through the transformed IAS against a float64 CPU reference on the moved triangle, 5 matrices x 6 rays = 30 comparisons, 30 match (t within 2e-5, u and v within 2e-5, primitive 0, misses where the reference misses; rotated triangles make "moved2" miss for rz37, rz90s2, rx37 and snu).
- Register image: the 32 bit words of lane 0 (ray hit) are identical for all five matrices except the two words that hold this process's IAS address (those two also differ between two identity runs). So the ray words handed to the unit do not depend on the matrix: the matrix is only in the instance leaf the unit reads.
- Predictions: check_seq pass and equal bytes: held (same group copied from the same dump by the same builder; I did not diff the bytes against an earlier build because the earlier /dev/shm builds are gone, the same dump and builder were used); words that change: none but addresses (held); OptiX equals CPU on all hits (held).

## 1b. Opacity micromaps, heavier any hit (addendum run)
ahw 256 (256 dependent FMA pairs added to every any hit call; predictions in addendum 2): A over D unchanged at 1.54 (N 100,000) and 1.60 (N 1,000,000), B(2), B(4), B(6) over A 1.01 to 1.04, B(8) 1.09, C(L) the same as before (0.77, 0.93, 1.02, 1.11), process to process spread 2 to 9 percent. So the any hit work is NOT on the critical path at this load: 256 times more math in the program changed nothing, which is why the micromap cannot win: the unit side lookup costs as much as it removes and the SM side program was hidden anyway. All predictions of addendum 2 missed (A over D growing, B(4) below 0.55 of A). Not tested: an any hit that does dependent memory loads (a real texture), where the program latency would be exposed. ## 2. Concurrent kernels (Conc_1)
Our checked TTU kernel (lean build, 20 warps per block, R 96) on SMs 0, 2, ..., 2G-2 beside a plain persistent compute kernel (4 blocks of 384 threads on every placed SM), 5 repetitions in the same order, each run in its own process, RT records checked against OptiX (0 wrong, 0 unstored in every run), compute rate against its own alone rate in the same process. RT G 32 (hi: compute on SMs 64 to 127, other TPCs; sib: compute on the odd SMs 1 to 63, the TPC siblings of the RT SMs) and G 64 (split: compute on all 64 odd SMs, siblings, as F101). RT speed against RT alone (RT spread over repetitions 0.2 to 5.0 percent; compute spread 0.0 to 4.8 percent):

|compute|random rays hi / sib / split|coherent rays hi / sib / split|compute side, random (hi / sib / split)|compute side, coherent|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|alu|1.077 / 1.080 / 1.063|1.070 / 1.071 / 1.069|0.998 / 0.999 / 0.997|0.999 / 0.999 / 0.999|
|l1 (32 KB per block spills)|0.721 / 0.370 / 0.494|0.764 / 0.835 / 0.796|0.626 / 0.586 / 0.573|0.680 / 0.547 / 0.522|
|mem (1 GB stream)|0.147 / 0.022 / 0.022|0.277 / 0.464 / 0.385|0.863 / 0.946 / 0.950|0.748 / 0.564 / 0.654|

- Memory streaming beside random rays: RT falls to 0.022 on the sibling SMs (same as F101's 0.02 on triton) but only to 0.147 when the compute kernel runs on other TPCs, although that compute kernel has twice the blocks (256 against 128). So the sibling placement costs RT 6.7 times more than the same traffic from other TPCs: the TPC shared path (the RT core and its memory port belong to the pair) matters on top of the chip wide L2 and DRAM path.
- For coherent rays the order flips: 0.277 (other TPCs) against 0.464 (siblings), and the compute side loses more when the sibling RT is coherent (0.564) than other TPCs (0.748).
- The 32 KB per block "L1" compute is not an L1 only load (4 blocks of 32 KB spill): it slows RT beside other TPCs too (0.72), and RT slows it back (0.57 to 0.68).
- The ALU kernel beside RT makes RT about 7 percent FASTER in every placement and both scenes (clock or power state suspected, not tested): so ratios for the other types are against an alone run that may be 7 percent slow; the compute alu rate is unchanged.
- Compute side with mem: RT random rays take little (0.86 to 0.95), coherent rays more (0.56 to 0.75).
- Scoring of predictions: mem random 0.02 to 0.2 held for both, but "within 25 percent of each other" missed (6.7 times); mem coherent 0.3 to 0.8: sib and split held, hi missed (0.277); l1 sib 0.7 to 1.0: held for coherent (0.835), missed for random (0.370); l1 hi 0.95 to 1.05 missed (0.72 and 0.76); alu 0.95 to 1.05 missed (1.06 to 1.08, faster); compute mem 0.85 to 1.0: held for random rays, missed for coherent (0.56 to 0.75). RT block SMs are not stored by the lean build: they follow the placement rule (probe_placement) and the compute kernel's resident counts printed per run (hi: 256 blocks on SMs 64 to 127 and none below; sib: 128 on SMs 1 to 63 odd).

## 3. Divergence profile (Div_1)
Probe: lanes active at every intersection (kNN also any hit) callback by popcount of the active mask, per warp bins (ttu/bench/Div_1/lanehist.h), warp time raygen start to the latest raygen end of any lane. First version underestimated warp time (only lane 0 stamped the end: some shares exceeded 1; those raw files are in notes/div1/raw_v1, not used); the lane stamp fix was made before the runs reported here. Datasets are synthetic (the real ones are on triton only): uniform 100,000 points (RTNN r 0.025 and 0.05, K 50; own kNN K 8 start radius 0.02 with doubling; TrueKNN k 16 start radius 0.004 at 100,000 and 400,000 points), Arkade fixed (float distance, device buffer) on 100,000 2D points with 10,000 queries at r 0.005 and 0.0112, Mochi frame 0 (100,000 particles, first launch of frame 0; later launches have no usable time column). 3 repeats each, one process per repeat; lane statistics are identical between repeats (spread 0.0 percent) and the time shares vary at most 1.2 percent (TrueKNN rounds up to 3). - Every callback is its own handoff: callbacks per handoff is 1.00 in all workloads (the gap rule of 300 cycles never merged two callbacks), so handoff lanes equal callback lanes. In these programs one callback follows one unit round trip.
- Mean lanes per callback are 7 to 19 of 32, below all my predictions except TrueKNN: lane efficiency 0.22 (TrueKNN 100,000) to 0.60 (Mochi frame 0). Only Mochi has more than half its callbacks at full width (56 percent at 32 lanes). Everywhere else 45 to 79 percent of callbacks run with 8 or fewer lanes.
- Time: 22 to 48 percent of warp time is spent in callback intervals with 8 or fewer lanes (Arkade 0.45 to 0.48, RTNN 0.33 to 0.35, own kNN 0.32, TrueKNN 0.22 to 0.30), 10 percent for Mochi. The intervals include the unit round trip before each callback (intervals sum to 94 to 99.8 percent of warp time), so this is warp time with few lanes returning together, not SM issue time; callback cycles themselves are 8 percent (Mochi) to 74 percent (TrueKNN) of warp time.
- Per round TrueKNN (100,000 points): mean lanes 14.2, 11.8, 13.2, 17.3 in rounds 1 to 4 and 1.8 in the last round (2,574 of 3,125 warps have callbacks but few lanes); kNN round 3 is one warp, one lane, 73 callbacks, and 97 percent of that warp's time is at one lane.
- Scoring: held: Mochi lowest few lane time; Arkade lane count in the wide band only at the low end (10 to 11 against 14 to 24: missed); RTNN mean 22 to 27: missed (12 to 14); kNN round 1 mean 24 to 30: missed (10.3); TrueKNN mean 8 to 18: held for the pooled run at 400,000 (12.7) and 100,000 (7.1, just below); few lane time ranking TrueKNN highest: missed (Arkade highest); Mochi under 5 percent: missed (9.7); handoffs below callbacks for Arkade: missed (1.00); probe overhead under 15 percent: held for Arkade (1.03 and 1.12, kernel time) and missed for RTNN (1.23 and 1.28 kernel time with the lane probe on); not measured for the others (their printed totals include the dump). Lane counts do not depend on timing, so the lane distributions stand; the time shares were measured with this perturbation.
- Notes: Mochi frame 0 launches 2 to 400 have the same lane distribution as launch 1 (19.2, identical to 4 digits). The own kNN any hit callbacks have 7.5 to 10.5 lanes (fewer than intersection). Not done: real datasets (triton), the original (managed memory) variants.

## 6. Build time and many instances (Struct_2; model/struct2_analyze.py, raw notes/struct2 private)
Design flaw disclosed: the instanced scenes keep the total triangle count (1,000,000) but small M leaves the cube only partly covered (hit fraction 0.56 at M 16, 0.73 at 256, 0.83 at 4,096, 0.88 at 65,536, 0.92 flat), so trace times at small M are lower because rays miss, not because instancing is cheaper. A first version with translations of the full range had even lower coverage (kept in notes/struct2/inst_v1). Only M 1 (identical scene) and M 65,536 (hit 0.88 against 0.92) compare cleanly.
- Build (4090, medians of 15 builds, 3 processes, N 1,000,000 / 100,000): none 1.89 / 0.44 ms, prefer fast trace 2.25 / 0.49 (1.19 and 1.11 times), prefer fast build 1.16 / 0.32 (0.61, 0.72), allow update 1.36 / 0.38 (0.72, 0.85: the update flag builds faster, F222's no spatial splits), refit 0.32 / 0.07 ms (0.17 of the build), allow compaction build 1.81 / 0.48 plus the compaction pass 0.134 / 0.030 ms (7 percent of the build), size 49.0 MB against 65.1 (-25 percent). Sizes 65.1, 70.0, 56.2, 65.3 MB (none, trace, build, update). Process spread 0.1 to 9 percent. 1M triangles are built in 1.9 ms (about 530 million triangles per second); a tenfold larger scene takes 4.3 times longer (the small build does not fill the GPU).
- Instances: M 1 (one instance, same scene as the flat GAS): trace time 1.00 of flat, but SM warp instructions 31.4 M against 47.0 M (the single level instancing pipeline runs 33 percent fewer SM instructions than the single GAS pipeline for the same traversal; sectors equal, 233 M). M 65,536 (T 15 triangles each): time 0.99 of flat at hit fraction 0.88 against 0.92, sectors +19 percent (280 M against 234 M), warp instructions 31.5 M. M 16 to 4,096: times 0.27 to 0.64 of flat but not comparable (coverage). IAS build time is 2.4 to 3.0 ms at every M from 1 to 65,536 (fixed, probably a first use cost of the instance build in the process; not separated). Memory: M 65,536 sums to 11.4 MB for 1,000,000 triangles (the 15 triangle GAS is shared by all instances, so far below the flat 65 MB).
- Scoring: build times missed (I predicted 8 to 30 ms and 0.8 to 4 ms; measured 1.9 and 0.44); fast trace 1.2 to 2.5 times: just missed (1.19, 1.11); fast build 0.5 to 0.85: held; refit 0.05 to 0.3: held; compaction under 15 percent: held. Instances: M 65,536 3 to 12 times flat: missed (0.99); the unit sectors above 2 times flat: missed (+19 percent); IAS build under 3 ms at 65,536: held; under 0.5 ms at 4,096: missed (2.5); memory at least 1.2 times flat at 4,096: missed (instances share one GAS).

## 7. Partial warps (Part_1; same program, raw notes/struct2)
2,097,152 traced rays in total on the 1,000,000 triangle soup; with A active lanes per warp the launch has 32/A times more threads (65,536 to 67,108,864); inactive lanes return at once. All checksums of the hits are identical across A and lane layout (same answers). Time per launch and per traced ray against A 32 (1.13 ms, spread 12 percent in this cell, so the baseline is noisy): A 16 1.03 and 1.05 (consecutive lanes, every second lane), A 8 1.15 and 1.16, A 1 5.4 (consecutive) and 5.1 (spread). ncu warp instructions: 47 M, 94 M, 185 M and 1,418 M (the SM issues 30 times more warp instructions at A 1 for 5 times the time), thread instructions 1.4 G, 1.8 G, 2.6 G, 13.0 G, sectors 234 to 265 M (almost constant).
- So a warp with 16 active lanes traces the same rays as a full warp in the same time per ray (about 3 to 5 percent more), 8 lanes costs 15 percent more, and one lane per warp costs 5 times per traced ray: per WARP the unit finishes a one lane warp 5.9 times faster than a full warp, so a warp's latency falls with its lanes but not in proportion. Which lanes are active does not matter (consecutive against every n-th lane: within 6 percent).
- Scoring: all three cost predictions missed low (16 lanes 1.4 to 2.1 predicted, 1.03 to 1.05 measured; 8 lanes 2.5 to 4.2, 1.15; 1 lane 10 to 32, 5.4); the lane layout prediction (within 10 percent) held; the 1 lane warp "within 20 percent of the 32 lane warp in warps per second" missed (5.9 times more warps per second). Caveat: this is the OptiX program with all rays independent, 8 million rays in flight; it is not one warp alone.
