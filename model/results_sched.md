# Sched_1 and Sched_2 results (2026-11-17, tethys RTX 4090 GPU 1; predictions model/predictions_sched.md committed (6b7fe0a) before any run; scripts ttu/bench/Sched_1/sched1.cu, ttu/bench/Sched_2/run.py, model/analyze_sched1.py, model/analyze_sched2.py; raw notes/sched, private)

## Sched_1 (plain CUDA, no TTU, one block of up to 32 warps, clock read after each segment of FMAs, 40 launches per configuration, first dropped, no fault)
Numbers: one warp alone: 40.0 cycles per segment of 32 independent FMAs (17.0 per 8 FMAs, slope 0.96 cycles per FMA, so 1 FMA per cycle per scheduler, plus about 9 cycles of clock read and shared store per segment), 131.1 per 32 dependent FMAs (4 cycles each).
1. Warp to scheduler map: warps 0 and 1 (and 0,1,2,3) run at the alone speed (40.0 to 40.1); warps 0 and 4 slow each other (69.6 to 70.1). So warp id mod 4 picks the scheduler (held, S1a).
2. Two warps on one scheduler alternate at the finest grain I can see (mean run of same warp stamps 1.00 at 8 and 32 FMAs per segment) and share equally (70 per 32 FMA segment, 1.75 times alone, because the 9 cycle overhead overlaps). Three warps: 103 to 105 each (equal share). So the finest grain is a round robin, not greedy runs (S1f missed: I predicted mean run above 1.5).
3. Four or more warps on one scheduler (independent FMAs): NOT fair. The three highest warp ids run together in rotation (about 105 cycles per segment each, the three finish in about 6,700 cycles) and the others hardly progress until they finish; then the next three. Four warps (0,4,8,12): warps 12, 8, 4 at 105 per segment, warp 0 at 162 on average, its first segment end at 7,300 cycles. Eight warps: groups (28,24,20), then (16,12,8), then (4,0); the last warp's first instruction is 6,844 cycles after the first. Five and seven warps: the same top three first. The warp ids are the ids inside the block (assumed to follow the hardware warp slot). With more stalls (8 FMAs per segment, or dependent chains) the starvation is much smaller (four warps 45 to 48 cycles per 8 FMA segment for all, 4 warps 5: 54 to 67); with 64 FMAs per segment the rotating group is two warps (172 cycles per segment), not three. So the group size is not a constant of the scheduler in these data; consistent with a scheduler that stays on the highest priority issuable warps and passes the slot on only when they stall (or hand it off by a yield hint), priority rising with warp id. Mechanism NOT identified.
4. Dependent chains (each warp issues every 4 cycles): one and two warps per scheduler at the alone speed (131.1 and 131.1), four warps 145 to 146 (1.11), eight warps 266 to 301 per segment (2.0 to 2.3 times); all warps progress (release spread about 140 cycles), so the order matters only when the scheduler is full.
5. Release order after the block barrier: with two warps on a scheduler the lowest id stamps first (39 of 39 launches, 2 cycles apart); with three or more the HIGHEST id stamps first (39 of 39) and the lowest ids are the ones that wait.
Scoring of the predictions: S1a held; S1b: slope held (1 FMA per cycle), absolute segment times missed (40 and 17 against 32 to 36 and 8 to 12: my design adds a 9 cycle overhead); S1c missed numerically (1.75 not 1.9 to 2.1; four warps not equal); S1d: two warps held, four warps 1.11 (just outside 0.95 to 1.10), eight warps 2.0 to 2.3 (against 2.0, partly held); S1e MISSED (I predicted lowest id first; it is the lowest id only for two warps, the highest id for three or more); S1f missed (strict alternation, not greedy runs, when the group is two or three warps; the unfairness is across groups).
Caveats: one SM at a time (blocks land on any SM, all results from SM 0 in these launches); one code shape (FFMA streams with a clock read and a shared store between segments; the compiler sets the yield and stall bits, not read here); 1024 thread blocks only; no other warps or blocks on the SM; plain FP32 FMA only; the starvation could be specific to a stream with no stalls (real kernels stall on memory).

## Sched_2 (RT core return priority): NOT TESTED, the run was invalid
The checked Unit_1 builds (check_seq ALL PASS) ran one TTU warp with and without the harness ALU compute kernel, 5 repetitions of 30 launches in rotating order, no fault: latency 3,586 cycles alone, 3,590 with "compute" (stage 0, same instructions with no TTU instruction: 80 and 80, identical in every launch). But the compute kernel was NOT running during any measured launch: the harness reports compute iterations per launch window, 0 for every launch after the first, and the first launch took 24.3 seconds (the 25 second watchdog of the compute kernel) with 1.3 billion iterations counted: the TTU block did not start until the compute kernel was stopped. (Tried grid multiplier 1 and 3 in a copy: same; cause not found; Finding 87 and F92 had overlap with large RT grids.) So both "alu" arms are the same measurement as the alone arms, and the +4 cycles is noise. The prediction S2a (0 to 40 cycles) is not scored. A second design is needed: either find why a one block TTU kernel waits behind the persistent compute kernel, or put the ready ALU warps in the same block as the TTU warp (needs a harness variant with ALU only warps in a TTU block; not built).
